🚀 Class 12 Physics Cheat Sheet

Visual & Animated Revision (Definitions + Solved Examples + Bilingual Real-Life Examples)

⚡ Unit 1: Electrostatics

Electric Charges and Fields

Coulomb's Law: Force between two point charges: F = k(q₁q₂)/r².
Gauss's Law: Total electric flux Φ = q / ε₀.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Electric Charge: An intrinsic property of matter due to which it produces and experiences electrical and magnetic effects. SI unit: Coulomb (C).
  • Quantisation of Charge: All free charges are integral multiples of a basic unit of charge denoted by e: q = ne, where e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C.
  • Electric Field (E): The electrostatic force experienced by a unit positive test charge placed at a point, E = F/q. SI unit: N/C or V/m.
  • Electric Flux: The total number of electric field lines passing normally through a given surface area, Φ = EA cosθ.
  • Electric Dipole Moment: Product of magnitude of either charge and the separation between them, p = q × 2a. Direction: from negative to positive charge.
💡 Example: If two charges of 1 μC each are 10 cm apart, F = 9×10⁹ × (10⁻⁶)² / (0.1)² = 0.9 N (repulsion). Flux through a closed surface enclosing 1 nC charge is Φ = q/ε₀ ≈ 113 N·m²/C.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Rubbing a plastic comb on dry hair charges it through friction, allowing it to attract tiny bits of paper via electrostatic force. Lightning during thunderstorms also occurs due to heavy charge accumulation in clouds. हिंदी में: प्लास्टिक की कंघी को सूखे बालों पर रगड़ने से वह आवेशित हो जाती है और कागज़ के छोटे टुकड़ों को खींच लेती है। यही स्थिरवैद्युत बल है। बादलों में आवेश जमा होने पर आकाशीय बिजली भी इसी कारण गिरती है।
Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Potential (V): Work done per unit charge.
Capacitor: Stores electrical energy (U = ½CV²). Dielectric increases capacitance: C = Kε₀A/d.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Electrostatic Potential: Work done by an external force in bringing a unit positive charge from infinity to a point in an electric field, V = W/q. SI unit: Volt (J/C).
  • Potential Difference: Work done per unit positive test charge in moving it from one point to another against electrostatic force.
  • Equipotential Surface: A surface with a constant value of potential at all points on it. No work is done in moving a test charge on this surface.
  • Capacitance: Ratio of the charge Q given to a conductor to the rise in its potential V, C = Q/V. SI unit: Farad (F).
  • Dielectric: A non-conducting substance that gets polarised when placed in an external electric field. Dielectric constant K = C/C₀.
💡 Example: When a 10 μF capacitor is charged to 100 V, charge Q = CV = 1 mC and stored energy U = ½ × 10×10⁻⁶ × 100² = 0.05 J.
🌍 Real-Life Example: In a camera or mobile flash, a capacitor charges up slowly and then releases all its stored energy in a fraction of a second to produce a bright flash. Ceiling fans also use a capacitor to provide starting torque. हिंदी में: मोबाइल/कैमरे के फ्लैश में संधारित्र धीरे-धीरे आवेशित होकर ऊर्जा जमा करता है और फिर एक ही पल में सारी ऊर्जा छोड़कर तेज़ रोशनी देता है। पंखे की स्टार्टिंग में भी संधारित्र लगता है।

🔋 Unit 2: Current Electricity

Drift Velocity and Ohm's Law

Drift Velocity: Average velocity of electrons in a conductor (I = nAev_d).
Ohm's Law: Potential difference is directly proportional to current (V = IR).

📖 Important Definitions
  • Electric Current: The rate of flow of electric charge through any cross-section of a conductor, I = Q/t. SI unit: Ampere (A).
  • Drift Velocity: The average velocity with which free electrons get drifted towards the positive end of a conductor under an external electric field, v_d = eEτ/m (τ = relaxation time).
  • Mobility (μ): The magnitude of the drift velocity per unit electric field, μ = v_d/E.
  • Electrical Resistance: Opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of current, R = ρl/A. SI unit: Ohm (Ω).
  • Resistivity (ρ): Resistance of a material of unit length and unit area of cross-section. It depends on the nature of material and temperature, not dimensions.
💡 Example: If 2 A current flows through a 5 Ω wire, V = IR = 2 × 5 = 10 V, and charge flowing in 5 s is Q = It = 10 C.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Compare electricity to water in a pipe: Voltage = water pressure, Current = water flow, Resistance = narrowness of the pipe. Heating coils in irons and room heaters have high resistance so they get hot, whereas house wiring uses thick copper wires for low resistance. हिंदी में: पानी की पाइप से समझें: वोल्टेज = पानी का दबाव, धारा = पानी का बहाव, प्रतिरोध = पाइप की तंगी। इसीलिए हीटर या प्रेस का तार उच्च प्रतिरोध का होता है और गर्म होता है, जबकि घर की वायरिंग मोटे तांबे के तार से की जाती है।
Kirchhoff's Rules

Junction Rule (KCL): Total current entering a junction equals total current leaving it.
Loop Rule (KVL): Algebraic sum of changes in potential around any closed loop is zero.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Electromotive Force (emf): Potential difference between the positive and negative electrodes of a cell in an open circuit (when no current is drawn). SI unit: Volt (V).
  • Internal Resistance (r): Resistance offered by the electrolyte of a cell to the flow of current inside it. Terminal voltage V = ε − Ir.
  • Junction: Any point in an electric circuit where two or more conductors meet. KCL is based on conservation of charge.
  • Wheatstone Bridge: Under balanced condition, no current flows through the galvanometer, P/Q = R/S. KVL is based on conservation of energy.
💡 Example: If 5 A enters a junction and 3 A goes into one branch, the other branch carries 5 − 3 = 2 A (KCL). For a 12 V cell with 4 Ω and 2 Ω in series, I = 12/(4+2) = 2 A (KVL).
🌍 Real-Life Example: When a river splits into two streams, total water remains conserved (KCL). Similarly, at a home switchboard, main current splits among parallel appliances like fans, bulbs, and TV. Just like climbing a hill and returning to the starting point gives zero net height change, net potential change in a closed loop is zero (KVL). हिंदी में: नदी जब दो धाराओं में बँटती है तो कुल पानी वही रहता है (KCL)। इसी तरह घर के बोर्ड पर पंखा, बल्ब और टीवी समांतर जुड़े होते हैं और मुख्य तार की धारा उनमें बँट जाती है। (KVL की तरह पहाड़ पर चढ़कर वापस उसी जगह आने पर कुल ऊँचाई-परिवर्तन शून्य होता है।)

🧲 Unit 3: Magnetic Effects of Current

Biot-Savart & Ampere's Circuital Law

Biot-Savart Law: Magnetic field due to a current element.
Ampere's Law: ∮ B · dl = μ₀I. Used to find field inside a solenoid.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Magnetic Field (B): Space around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic force is experienced. SI unit: Tesla (T).
  • Magnetic Lorentz Force: Force acting on a charge q moving with velocity v in a magnetic field B, F = qvB sinθ.
  • Biot-Savart Law: Magnetic field due to a small current element dB = (μ₀/4π) · I dl sinθ / r².
  • Solenoid: A long cylindrical coil of insulated wire. Inside a long solenoid, field is uniform: B = μ₀nI.
  • Circular Loop (at centre): B = μ₀I/2R.
💡 Example: For a long straight wire carrying 5 A, magnetic field at 10 cm distance is B = μ₀I/2πr = 2×10⁻⁷ × 5 / 0.1 = 10⁻⁵ T.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Placing a magnetic compass near a live electric wire deflects its needle (Oersted's experiment). Scrapyard cranes use electromagnets (solenoids) to lift heavy iron when current is switched on and drop it when switched off. हिंदी में: बिजली के तार के पास कंपास रखने पर उसकी सुई घूम जाती है (ओर्स्टेड का प्रयोग)। क्रेन में लगा विद्युत चुंबक (परिनालिका की तरह) कबाड़खाने में बिजली चालू करने पर लोहे को उठाता है और बंद करने पर छोड़ देता है।
Moving Coil Galvanometer & Magnetism

Measures small electric currents. Works on the principle that a current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences a torque (τ = NIAB sinθ). Earth behaves like a giant magnet (declination, dip, horizontal component).

📖 Important Definitions
  • Magnetic Dipole Moment: Product of current and area of a current loop, m = NIA.
  • Current Sensitivity: Deflection produced per unit current in a galvanometer, θ/I = NBA/k.
  • Ammeter / Voltmeter: Galvanometer converts to an ammeter by connecting a low shunt resistance in parallel, and to a voltmeter by connecting a high resistance in series.
  • Magnetic Declination: Angle between geographic meridian and magnetic meridian at a place.
  • Angle of Dip (Inclination): Angle that Earth's total magnetic field makes with the horizontal direction.
  • Diamagnetic / Paramagnetic / Ferromagnetic: Materials weakly repelled, weakly attracted, and strongly attracted by a magnetic field respectively.
💡 Example: A 100-turn coil (area 0.01 m², current 2 A) placed perpendicular to a 0.5 T field experiences torque τ = NIAB = 100 × 2 × 0.01 × 0.5 = 1 N·m.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Electric fans, mixer grinders, and e-rickshaw motors run on this principle: a current-carrying coil inside a magnetic field experiences torque and rotates. Old analog needle meters also work this way. हिंदी में: बिजली का पंखा, मिक्सर और ई-रिक्शा की मोटर इसी सिद्धांत पर चलते हैं: चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में धारावाही कुंडली पर बल आघूर्ण लगता है और वह घूमने लगती है। पुराने एनालॉग मीटर की सुई भी इसी से घूमती है।

🔄 Unit 4: Electromagnetic Induction & AC

Electromagnetic Induction (EMI)

Faraday's Law: Changing magnetic flux induces an EMF (e = -dΦ/dt).
Lenz's Law: Induced EMF opposes the cause that produces it.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Magnetic Flux: Total number of magnetic field lines passing normally through a surface, Φ = BA cosθ. SI unit: Weber (Wb).
  • Electromagnetic Induction: Phenomenon of generating an induced emf in a circuit by changing the magnetic flux linked with it.
  • Motional EMF: EMF induced across the ends of a conductor moving in a magnetic field, e = Blv.
  • Self-Inductance (L): Property of a coil by which it opposes any change in current flowing through itself, e = −L dI/dt. SI unit: Henry (H).
  • Mutual Inductance (M): Induction of emf in a secondary coil due to change of current in a neighbouring primary coil.
  • Eddy Currents: Circulating currents induced in bulk pieces of conductors when subjected to changing magnetic flux (e.g., induction cooktop).
💡 Example: A 0.5 m rod moving at 2 m/s perpendicular to a 0.2 T field develops e = Blv = 0.2 × 0.5 × 2 = 0.2 V.
🌍 Real-Life Example: A bicycle dynamo spins a magnet near a coil using wheel rotation to light the headlamp. Power plants rotate huge coils via turbines to generate electricity, while induction cooktops use eddy currents to heat metal vessels directly. हिंदी में: साइकिल का डायनेमो पहिये के घूमने से चुंबक घुमाकर बल्ब जलाता है। बिजलीघर में टरबाइन से कुंडली घुमाकर बिजली बनती है, और इंडक्शन चूल्हा भँवर धाराओं से बर्तन को सीधे गर्म करता है।
Alternating Current (AC) & Transformer

LCR Circuit: Resonance occurs when inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance (X_L = X_C).
Transformer: Steps up or steps down AC voltage using mutual induction: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Alternating Current: Current whose magnitude changes continuously with time and direction reverses periodically.
  • RMS Value: Effective value of AC that produces the same heat as a steady DC, I_rms = I₀/√2 ≈ 0.707 I₀.
  • Reactance: Opposition offered by inductor X_L = ωL or capacitor X_C = 1/ωC to AC.
  • Impedance (Z): Total effective opposition of an LCR circuit, Z = √(R² + (X_L − X_C)²).
  • Power Factor: Ratio cosφ = R/Z. Average power P = V_rms I_rms cosφ.
  • Resonant Frequency: f₀ = 1/(2π√LC).
  • Step-Up / Step-Down Transformer: Increases voltage when N_s > N_p; decreases voltage when N_s < N_p.
💡 Example: If peak household AC voltage is 311 V, V_rms = 311/√2 ≈ 220 V. A transformer with N_p = 1000, N_s = 100 and 220 V input gives V_s = 22 V (Step-Down).
🌍 Real-Life Example: A mobile charger steps down 220 V household AC to around 5 V. Power stations transmit electricity over long distances at very high voltage to reduce heat loss in wires, and local neighbourhood transformers step it back down to 220 V. हिंदी में: मोबाइल चार्जर घर की 220 V AC को लगभग 5 V में बदलता है (स्टेप-डाउन)। बिजलीघर से शहर तक बिजली बहुत ऊँची वोल्टता पर भेजी जाती है ताकि तार में ऊर्जा हानि कम हो, और मोहल्ले का ट्रांसफॉर्मर उसे 220 V करता है।

📡 Unit 5: Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic Spectrum

Transverse waves of oscillating electric and magnetic fields travelling at speed of light (c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀)).
Order (increasing frequency): Radio → Microwave → Infrared → Visible → UV → X-rays → Gamma.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Displacement Current: Current arising due to a time-varying electric flux, I_d = ε₀ dΦ_E/dt (Maxwell).
  • Electromagnetic Wave: Transverse wave in which electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields oscillate perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation. Requires no material medium.
  • Speed in Vacuum: c = 3×10⁸ m/s = fλ.
  • Main Uses: Radio (communication), Microwaves (radar/ovens), IR (TV remotes), UV (sterilisation), X-rays (medical imaging), Gamma rays (cancer therapy).
💡 Example: For an FM radio station broadcasting at 100 MHz, wavelength λ = c/f = 3×10⁸ / 10⁸ = 3 m.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Microwave ovens heat food, TV remotes use infrared rays, Wi-Fi and mobile networks run on radio/microwaves, hospitals use X-rays to check bone fractures, and UV water purifiers kill germs in drinking water. हिंदी में: माइक्रोवेव ओवन खाना गर्म करता है, टीवी रिमोट इन्फ्रारेड से चलता है, Wi-Fi और मोबाइल सिग्नल रेडियो/माइक्रोवेव तरंगों से काम करते हैं, अस्पताल में हड्डी की जाँच X-रे से होती है, और RO/UV वाटर प्यूरीफायर में UV कीटाणु मारती है।

🌈 Unit 6: Optics

Ray Optics (TIR and Lenses)

TIR: Occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium with angle of incidence > critical angle.
Lens Maker's Formula: 1/f = (n-1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂).

📖 Important Definitions
  • Refractive Index (n): Ratio of speed of light in vacuum to speed of light in the medium, n = c/v = sin i / sin r.
  • Total Internal Reflection (TIR): Complete reflection of light back into the denser medium when incident at an angle greater than the critical angle. Uses: optical fibres, sparkling of diamonds.
  • Critical Angle (C): Angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is 90°, sin C = 1/n.
  • Focal Length (f): Distance between the optical centre and principal focus of a lens.
  • Power of a Lens: Reciprocal of focal length in metres, P = 1/f. SI unit: Dioptre (D).
  • Lens Formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.
💡 Example 1: For glass (n = 1.5), critical angle sin C = 1/1.5 → C ≈ 42°.
💡 Example 2: An object placed 30 cm from a convex lens of f = +20 cm forms an image at 1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60 → v = 60 cm.
🌍 Real-Life Example: High-speed internet optical fibre cables carry light signals over long distances using TIR. Diamonds sparkle brilliantly due to TIR, and summer road mirages also happen because of it. Spectacles, cameras, and magnifying glasses work on the lens formula. हिंदी में: इंटरनेट की ऑप्टिकल फाइबर केबल में प्रकाश पूर्ण आंतरिक परावर्तन से मुड़ता हुआ लंबी दूरी तय करता है। हीरा इसी कारण चमकता है। गर्मी में सड़क पर पानी जैसा दिखना (मरीचिका) भी TIR है। चश्मे, कैमरे और आवर्धक लेंस सब लेंस सूत्र पर काम करते हैं।
Wave Optics

Huygens' Principle: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary wavelets.
Interference: Superposition of light waves forming bright and dark fringes (β = λD/d).

📖 Important Definitions
  • Wavefront: Locus of all points in a medium vibrating in the same phase at a given instant.
  • Coherent Sources: Two sources emitting light waves of the same frequency with a constant phase difference over time.
  • Constructive Interference: Occurs when path difference is nλ, giving maximum intensity (bright fringe).
  • Destructive Interference: Occurs when path difference is (2n−1)λ/2, giving minimum intensity (dark fringe).
  • Diffraction: Bending of light around the sharp corners of an obstacle or narrow aperture.
  • Polarisation: Restricting vibrations of light waves to a single plane (proves light is transverse). Brewster's Law: tan θ_B = n.
💡 Example: In Young's Double Slit Experiment (YDSE), if λ = 600 nm, D = 1 m, and d = 1 mm, fringe width β = λD/d = 6×10⁻⁷ × 1 / 10⁻³ = 0.6 mm.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Rainbow colours seen on soap bubbles or thin oil films on water are caused by interference, while colourful patterns on a CD/DVD come from diffraction. Polarised sunglasses block reflected glare from wet roads. हिंदी में: साबुन के बुलबुले या पानी पर तेल की परत पर दिखने वाले इंद्रधनुषी रंग और CD/DVD पर रंगीन चमक व्यतिकरण/विवर्तन के कारण होते हैं। धूप के पोलराइज़्ड चश्मे सड़क की चकाचौंध (ध्रुवित प्रकाश) को रोकते हैं।

☀️ Unit 7: Dual Nature of Radiation & Matter

Photoelectric Effect & de Broglie Waves

Einstein's Equation: K_max = hν - Φ.
de Broglie Wavelength: Wave nature of moving particles λ = h/p = h/mv.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Photoelectric Effect: Emission of electrons from a metal surface when light of suitable frequency falls on it.
  • Threshold Frequency (ν₀): Minimum frequency of incident light below which no photoelectric emission takes place.
  • Work Function (Φ): Minimum energy required by an electron to just escape from a metal surface, Φ = hν₀.
  • Stopping Potential (V₀): Minimum negative anode potential at which photoelectric current becomes zero, eV₀ = K_max.
  • Photon: Discrete packet (quantum) of light energy, E = hν.
  • Matter Wave: Wave associated with a moving material particle. For an electron accelerated through V volts, λ = 12.27/√V Å.
💡 Example 1: If a 5 eV photon hits a metal with work function Φ = 2 eV, K_max = 5 − 2 = 3 eV and stopping potential V₀ = 3 V.
💡 Example 2: Wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V is λ = 12.27/√100 ≈ 1.23 Å.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Automatic doors in malls and lifts use light sensors: when a person interrupts the beam, current changes and the door opens. Solar panels generate electricity via the photoelectric effect, and electron microscopes use electron waves to view ultra-tiny objects. हिंदी में: मॉल या लिफ्ट के स्वचालित दरवाज़े में प्रकाश-सेंसर होता है: प्रकाश कटने पर धारा बदलती है और दरवाज़ा खुलता है। सोलर पैनल प्रकाश-विद्युत प्रभाव से बिजली बनाते हैं और इलेक्ट्रॉन सूक्ष्मदर्शी में इलेक्ट्रॉन की तरंग प्रकृति से बहुत छोटी चीज़ें दिखती हैं।

⚛️ Unit 8: Atoms and Nuclei

Bohr's Model and Hydrogen Spectrum

Electrons revolve only in stable orbits where angular momentum is quantized: mvr = nh/2π.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Rutherford's Nuclear Model: Entire positive charge and most of the mass of an atom are concentrated in a tiny central core called the nucleus, with electrons revolving around it.
  • Bohr's First Postulate: An electron revolves in certain stable orbits without radiating energy.
  • Bohr's Third Postulate: A photon is emitted or absorbed when an electron jumps between energy levels, hν = E_i − E_f.
  • Energy of Hydrogen Electron: E_n = −13.6/n² eV.
  • Spectral Series: Lyman (n→1, UV), Balmer (n→2, Visible), Paschen (n→3, IR).
💡 Example: During transition from n = 3 to n = 2, energy emitted is ΔE = 13.6 (1/4 − 1/9) ≈ 1.89 eV (first red line of Balmer series).
🌍 Real-Life Example: The orange-red glow of neon signs, yellow glow of sodium street lamps, and vibrant colours of fireworks happen when excited electrons jump back to lower orbits and emit specific photons. Astronomers identify elements in distant stars by studying their light spectrum. हिंदी में: नियॉन साइन का लाल-नारंगी रंग, सोडियम लैंप (स्ट्रीट लाइट) की पीली रोशनी और आतिशबाज़ी के अलग-अलग रंग इलेक्ट्रॉनों के ऊँची कक्षा से नीचे गिरने पर निकले विशिष्ट फोटॉन के कारण होते हैं। वैज्ञानिक तारों का स्पेक्ट्रम देखकर उनके तत्व पहचानते हैं।
Nuclei and Binding Energy

Mass Defect: Mass of a nucleus is always less than the total mass of its free protons and neutrons. This missing mass converts into Binding Energy (E = Δmc²) that holds the nucleus together.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Mass Number (A) / Atomic Number (Z): A = total protons + neutrons (nucleons), Z = number of protons.
  • Isotopes: Atoms having same Z but different A (e.g., ¹H, ²H). Isobars: Atoms having same A but different Z.
  • Binding Energy: Minimum energy required to separate a nucleus into its constituent nucleons. 1 u = 931.5 MeV.
  • Half-Life (T½): Time taken for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay, N = N₀ (½)^(t/T½).
  • Nuclear Fission / Fusion: Splitting of a heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei (nuclear reactors) / Combining of light nuclei into a heavier nucleus (energy source of the Sun).
💡 Example: Mass defect of ⁴He nucleus is 0.0304 u, so its Binding Energy is BE = 0.0304 × 931.5 ≈ 28.3 MeV.
🌍 Real-Life Example: Nuclear power plants generate electricity via controlled fission of uranium, where tiny mass converts into huge energy. The Sun shines due to nuclear fusion of hydrogen, and radioactive half-life is used in carbon dating to estimate the age of ancient fossils. हिंदी में: परमाणु बिजलीघर में यूरेनियम के विखंडन से थोड़ा द्रव्यमान ऊर्जा में बदलकर बिजली बनती है। सूर्य हाइड्रोजन के संलयन से चमकता है। रेडियोसक्रियता की अर्ध-आयु से पुरातात्विक वस्तुओं की उम्र (कार्बन डेटिंग) निकाली जाती है।

💻 Unit 9: Electronic Devices

Semiconductors and p-n Junction Diode

p-n Junction: Conducts current easily in forward bias. A rectifier converts AC into DC.

📖 Important Definitions
  • Semiconductor: Material whose conductivity lies between conductors and insulators (e.g., Si, Ge). Its resistance decreases as temperature increases.
  • n-type Semiconductor: Obtained by doping pure Si/Ge with pentavalent impurity (e.g., Phosphorus); electrons are majority carriers.
  • p-type Semiconductor: Obtained by doping pure Si/Ge with trivalent impurity (e.g., Boron); holes are majority carriers.
  • Depletion Region: Space-charge region near the p-n junction devoid of free charge carriers. Barrier potential: Si ≈ 0.7 V, Ge ≈ 0.3 V.
  • Forward / Reverse Bias: Connecting p-side to positive and n-side to negative is forward bias (current flows); reversing polarity gives reverse bias (negligible current).
  • Rectifier: Device that converts AC into pulsating DC — Half-wave (1 diode) and Full-wave (2 or 4 diodes).
💡 Example: A silicon diode (forward biased) connected in series with a 1 kΩ resistor and a 5 V battery carries current I = (5 − 0.7)/1000 = 4.3 mA.
🌍 Real-Life Example: A diode acts like a one-way traffic valve, allowing current to flow in only one direction. Mobile chargers use rectifiers to convert AC into DC, while LED bulbs, TV screens, and remote indicators work on special p-n junctions (LEDs). हिंदी में: डायोड एकतरफा वाल्व की तरह है: धारा सिर्फ एक दिशा में जाने देता है। मोबाइल चार्जर में दिष्टकारी AC को DC बनाता है और LED बल्ब, टीवी तथा रिमोट की रोशनी भी p-n संधि (LED) पर ही आधारित हैं।
